# M1-SRC-01 — targeted short reversal reading

Accessed October 6, 2026 for SR-003-M1-1. Primary sources, external findings only; no local result at preparation.

Stefan Nagel, *Evaporating Liquidity*, December 2011 author manuscript, 56 PDF pages: https://bpb-us-w2.wpmucdn.com/voices.uchicago.edu/dist/f/575/files/2020/07/LiqSupply.pdf . Read abstract, introduction (PDF1–7), empirical-proxy discussion (PDF12–20), especially lagged-return weights, closing-price versus quote-midpoint measurement and factor exposure. Not full proof/appendix replication. The author interprets short reversal returns as a liquidity-provision proxy, finds strong time variation and separates transaction-price bounce from midpoint effects. His strategy uses long/short positions, lag combinations and hedging; our one-day rank tilt, long-only shares, next-open entry and five-session holding are an adaptation, not his reported strategy. Neither lagged prices nor this test identifies actual inventories, nonfundamental news or executable liquidity profits. A liquid concentrated convenience cohort can behave differently. We do not import VIX conditioning, market hedges or his return estimates.

Avramov, Chordia and Goyal, *Liquidity and Autocorrelations in Individual Stock Returns*, June 22, 2004 author/conference manuscript, 45 PDF pages: https://users.nber.org/~confer/2004/mmsu04/goyal.pdf . Reread abstract/introduction and transaction-cost discussion (PDF19–21). Authors find reversal concentrated in high-turnover, illiquid losers, with trading costs capable of exceeding apparent gains. Their historical market/sample and cost estimates are not contemporary local calibrations. This counsels explicit assumed costs and against interpreting price reversals as tradeable alpha. Extends SRC-015 reading rather than claiming a new replication.
