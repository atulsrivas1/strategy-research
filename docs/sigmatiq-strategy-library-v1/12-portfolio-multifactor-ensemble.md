## 1. Origin & lineage

**Academic origins.**
- Value: Stattman (1980), Rosenberg-Reid-Lanstein (1985), Fama & French (1992) — book-to-market effect in US stocks.
- Momentum: Jegadeesh & Titman (1993), Asness (1994) — 3–12 month relative strength.
- Carry: currency carry trade literature (cited in Koijen et al. 2018); generalized to all assets by **Koijen, Moskowitz, Pedersen & Vrugt, "Carry," *JFE* 127(2), 2018** (read, §8).
- Quality: profitability (Novy-Marx 2013), low-risk (Black-Jensen-Scholes 1972; Frazzini & Pedersen 2014 BAB), formalized as a factor by **Asness, Frazzini & Pedersen, "Quality Minus Junk"** (working paper 2013; *Review of Accounting Studies* 2019) (read, §8).

**The integrator.** **Asness, Moskowitz & Pedersen, "Value and Momentum Everywhere," *Journal of Finance* 68(3), 2013** (read, §8) — the foundational multi-asset factor paper: value and momentum premia in **eight markets/asset classes** (US/UK/Europe/Japan stocks, country equity index futures, government bonds, currencies, commodities), with a common global factor structure. AQR (founded 1998 by Asness, Frazzini, Kabiller, Liew, Krail et al.) built its business on exactly this style-diversified, multi-asset long-short approach; Moskowitz, Ooi & Pedersen (2012, *JFE*) added time-series momentum across futures; Frazzini & Pedersen (2014) added betting-against-beta.

**The factor-timing / crowding debate (both sides read).**
- *AQR side:* Asness, "The Siren Song of Factor Timing," *JPM* special issue 2016 (read in full): valuation-based factor timing is "very weak historically," too correlated with the value factor itself, useful only at unprecedented extremes (1999–2000); advice: diversify across factors, "sin a little" if you time at all.
- *Research Affiliates side:* Arnott, Beck, Kalesnik & West (2016, "How Can 'Smart Beta' Go Horribly Wrong?" — the ABKW paper Asness rebuts); Arnott, Kalesnik & Wu (2017, "The Incredible Shrinking Factor Return" — read, §8): mutual-fund investors captured only ~60% of the theoretical value premium and **essentially zero** of the momentum premium 1991–2016; Arnott, Harvey, Kalesnik & Linnainmaa (2019, "Alice's Adventures in Factorland," SSRN abstract read): three blunders — data-mined/crowded expectations, downside shocks, vanishing diversification.

**How it is actually used.** AQR-style style-premia funds, risk-premia indices from banks, and multi-strategy pods all run versions of value+momentum+carry+defensive across futures. The live-implementation gap documented by Arnott-Kalesnik-Wu is the central practitioner caution. Asness's public commentary (the 2016 timing essay; his post-2007 and post-2020 letters) is the best record of how the largest practitioner thinks about surviving factor drawdowns: diversify across factors and asset classes, keep leverage survivable, monitor valuations for true extremes, and expect to be "painfully" wrong for stretches measured in years — a governance posture our circuit breaker and overlay design encode mechanically rather than discretionarily.

## 2. The original rules (as published)

### 2.1 Value and Momentum Everywhere (AMP 2013)

- **Universe:** individual stocks (US 1972–, UK/Europe/Japan 1974–; liquid large-cap-tilted universe — top ~90% of market cap); 18 country equity index futures (1978–); 10 currencies via forwards (1979–); 10 government bond markets (1982–); 27 commodity futures (1972–). All series end July 2011.
- **Signals (deliberately simple, uniform, anti-data-mining):**
  - Value, stocks: BE/ME, book lagged 6 months, current market value.
  - Value, equity indices: value-weighted aggregate BE/ME of constituents.
  - Value, currencies/bonds/commodities: **negative of the past 5-year return** (long-term reversal as value; for currencies, adjusted for local 3m LIBOR — a 5-year PPP deviation; for commodities, spot price 5 years ago over current spot). For bonds, alternative measures reported: real yield (10y yield − 5y inflation forecast) and term spread (10y − short rate) — Sharpe 0.73 and 0.55 vs 0.18 for the 5-year-return measure; a composite reaches 0.91–1.10.
  - Momentum, all assets: past 12-month cumulative return **skipping the most recent month** (MOM2-12). Skipping the last month is conservative for non-stock assets (results are stronger without the skip).
- **Portfolio construction:** (i) tercile sorts (high/middle/low), value-weighted for stocks, equal-weighted elsewhere; (ii) **signal-weighted long-short factors**: `w_i,t = c_t · (rank(S_i,t) − mean_rank(S_t))`, scaled to $1 long / $1 short — rank weights mitigate outliers and outperform coarse tercile spreads.
- **Combination:** `COMBO = 0.5·VALUE + 0.5·MOM` (equal 50/50).
- **Headline results (authors' claims):** value-momentum correlation ≈ **−0.60** within stock markets, **−0.54** within non-stock asset classes; value strategies correlate 0.68 across stock markets, momentum 0.65; the 50/50 combination applied across **all** asset classes produces an **annualized Sharpe of 1.42**; value in one asset class is negatively correlated with momentum in *other* asset classes; first principal component loads opposite on value vs momentum everywhere (54% of stock-strategy covariance). A global three-factor model (market + value-everywhere + momentum-everywhere) prices the cross-section; **funding liquidity risk** (post-1998 especially) is a partial driver — momentum loads positively, value negatively — but cannot explain the value premium or the combination.
- **Construction details worth copying exactly:** signals are z-scored ranks within each asset class; the rank-weighted factor `w_i = c·(rank(S_i) − mean_rank)` is self-financing by construction (weights sum to zero) and linear in signal rank, so no single outlier instrument can dominate — the authors show this beats tercile spread portfolios; stock portfolios are value-weighted within sorts, futures equal-weighted; all factors are scaled to a common ex-ante volatility before combination (the published precedent for our inverse-vol assembly).
- **What AMP explicitly leave out:** transaction costs (acknowledged; later addressed by Frazzini-Israel-Moskowitz 2012 and KMPV's net-of-cost checks), capacity, and any timing/regime conditioning — the paper is a *premia-existence* proof, not a tradeable product spec. Our §5 spec is the product layer the paper deliberately does not provide.

### 2.2 Carry (KMPV 2018)

- **Definition (model-free, ex ante):** for a futures contract, `C_t = (S_t − F_t)/X_t` — the return if the spot price is unchanged at expiry; fully collateralized scaling `X_t = F_t` gives `C_t = (S_t − F_t)/F_t`. Expected return decomposes as `E_t[r_{t+1}] = C_t + E_t[ΔS_{t+1}]/X_t`.
- **Asset-class interpretations:** currencies → interest-rate differential; bonds → yield-curve slope plus roll-down; commodities → basis/convenience yield; equities → forward-looking dividend yield (via index futures); also applied to US Treasuries across maturities, credit, and index options across moneyness.
- **Strategies:** cross-sectional long-short (rank on carry within each asset class) and **carry timing** (long when carry > 0 or above its historical mean).
- **Headline results (authors' claims):** carry predicts returns in every asset class; predictive coefficient > 1 in global equities/bonds/credit (carry also predicts price appreciation), < 1 in commodities/options (market takes back part of the carry); carry-timing Sharpe ratios average **0.6**, global carry-timing across all asset classes **0.9**; the diversified cross-asset carry factor has **Sharpe 1.2** with **negligible skewness** (kurtosis 5.40 — fat tails both ways); a **carry1-12** variant (average carry signal over the trailing 12 months) keeps Sharpe **1.1** while cutting turnover ~50%; net of realistic transaction costs (Bollerslev et al. 2016 estimates), returns remain positive and significant for equities, fixed income, Treasuries, commodities, currencies.
- **Risk exposures:** carry strategies lose in global recessions, are positively exposed to liquidity shocks and negatively to volatility risk; the three biggest global carry drawdowns — **Aug 1972–Sep 1975, Mar 1980–Jun 1982, Aug 2008–Feb 2009** — coincide with major macro events, and in those episodes *all* asset-class carry strategies underperform their own passive markets. US-Treasury-maturity carry is the exception (hedges the others).
- **Why "carry everywhere" matters for our spec:** KMPV's contribution is not a new signal but a *unifying definition* — the same `C = (S−F)/F` formula reduces to the interest differential in FX, slope+roll-down in bonds, basis in commodities, and implied dividend yield in equity indices. This means one code path computes carry for every futures sleeve, and the carry factor is *not* spanned by value, momentum, or TSMOM in their regressions — it is a genuinely additive premium stream, which is why it earns a sleeve of its own in FLEX-ENS rather than being folded into value.

### 2.3 Quality Minus Junk (AFP 2013/2019)

- **Quality score:** `Quality = z(Profitability + Growth + Safety + Payout)` — each component an average of z-scored (rank-standardized) measures:
  - Profitability: gross profits/assets (GPOA), ROE, ROA, cash flow/assets (CFOA), gross margin (GMAR), low accruals (ACC).
  - Growth: prior **5-year growth** in each profitability measure (change in numerator scaled by lagged denominator).
  - Safety: low beta (BAB), low idiosyncratic vol (IVOL), low leverage (LEV), low bankruptcy risk (O-score, Z-score), low ROE volatility (EVOL).
  - Payout: net equity issuance (EISS), net debt issuance (DISS), total net payout/profits (NPOP).
- **Factor:** long top 30% quality / short bottom 30% (junk), within large and small universes separately, Fama-French-style.
- **Headline results (authors' claims):** QMJ earns significant risk-adjusted returns in the US and is positive in **23 of 24** countries; QMJ has *negative* market, value, and size exposures, positive alpha, and does well in downturns ("mild positive convexity... benefits from flight to quality"); high-quality stocks are only modestly more expensive (the "puzzlingly modest" price of quality); the price of quality (cross-sectional P/B-on-quality regression coefficient) hit its low in **February 2000** and negatively predicts future QMJ returns; controlling for quality resurrects the size factor (SMB alpha 13bp/mo → 64bp/mo, t = 6.39). Sample: 39,308 stocks, 24 countries, US long sample from June 1956, broad sample 1986–2012.
- **Construction details worth copying exactly:** all quality measures are z-scored by rank within the universe and averaged with equal weight into the four components, then into the composite (no optimized weights); portfolios are formed within large-cap and small-cap universes separately and combined (avoids the factor becoming a disguised size bet); accounting data are aligned to avoid look-ahead (June of year t for fiscal year t−1 in the US convention); the price-of-quality series — our crowding proxy for the QUALITY sleeve — is the cross-sectional regression coefficient of log P/B on quality score, computed monthly: a *high* coefficient means investors are paying a lot for quality (crowded, low forward QMJ returns), a low/negative coefficient means quality is cheap.

### 2.4 The timing debate (both sides, as published)

- **Asness (2016):** value-spread timing (valuation of a factor's long side vs short side, AFKL 2000) is weak in normal times and "way too correlated to the basic value strategy itself to make great impact on a portfolio"; the only actionable case is *unprecedented* extremes (1999–2000); as of 2016, spreads were within historical norms (value looked cheap on book-to-price, near-normal on sales-to-price); factor crashes (Aug 2007) were **not** predicted by valuations and are "an unfortunate pain we must bear"; recommendation: believe in factors for the long haul, diversify aggressively, monitor spreads for true extremes, and if you time, "sin a little." He also rebuts ABKW's "factors were never real, just richening" argument as convoluted (overstating richening, ignoring wedges, misusing long-horizon regressions on high-turnover factors). Two of his specific points deserve permanent desk residency: (i) a timing signal that is correlated ~0.8+ with the value factor itself is not a new signal — it is leverage on the same bet with extra steps; (ii) the 1999–2000 exception proves the rule precisely because it was *unprecedented* — a timing rule that fires once in 40 years is a risk governor, not a return engine, which is exactly how our overlay (b) is designed (top decile, halve only, never zero).
- **Arnott-Kalesnik-Wu (2017):** comparing theoretical long-short factor returns to premia reverse-engineered from 5,323 US mutual funds (1991–2016, Fama-MacBeth two-pass): realized shortfall **−4.2%/yr for market** (t = −3.54), **−1.4%/yr for value** (managers captured 2.2% of the theoretical 3.6%), **−5.2%/yr for momentum** (realized ≈ 0), size +0.7% (not significant); correlations actual-vs-theoretical 0.89–0.96. Sources of slippage: trading costs, small/illiquid tilts, shorting costs, missed trades, fees, stale prices. Arnott, Harvey, Kalesnik & Linnainmaa (2019) add: backtest performance is partly **revaluation** (factors getting more expensive), which presages disappointment; diversification across factors vanishes in certain economic conditions.
- **Honest synthesis:** the two sides agree more than their tone suggests — Asness accepts monitoring for extremes and a possible small timing "sin"; Arnott accepts factors exist but insists valuation and implementation costs dominate naive expectations. Our twist adopts *bounded* regime/crowding conditioning (never all-in/all-out timing) — closer to Asness's "sin a little," with Arnott's crowding/valuation overlay as the risk governor.
- **Where the debate lands for a futures-based ensemble:** Arnott-Kalesnik-Wu's shortfall evidence is measured on *mutual funds* (long-biased, fee-bearing, small-cap-tilted implementations) — the worst-case implementation stack. A futures-based rank-weighted ensemble avoids shorting costs, most market-impact costs, and management-fee drag, so the AKW shortfall is a bound, not a prediction, for our implementation. But their deeper point stands independent of implementation: if a factor's historical return was substantially *revaluation* (the long side getting more expensive), the forward premium is mechanically lower than the backtest — which is exactly what overlay (b) monitors. The desk should treat the AKW numbers as the cost-model sanity check (§5) and the Arnott-Harvey-Kalesnik-Linnainmaa "blunders" as the design review checklist: data mining (counter: four premia with decades of out-of-sample and cross-asset replication), crowding (counter: overlay b), vanishing diversification (counter: leverage cap + circuit breaker, §6 item 7).

## 3. Why it works — mechanism & evidence

**Mechanisms (per the literature).**
1. **Value:** compensation for distress/gllamour-avoidance (risk story) or overreaction to past performance (behavioral story); AMP show value loads negatively on funding-liquidity risk — value does worst when arbitrage capital is scarce, a rational-risk component.
2. **Momentum:** underreaction/slow information diffusion and herding; AMP's crowding interpretation — momentum is the *popular* trade, hit hardest in liquidity spirals (positive funding-liquidity loading), which rationalizes its crashes (Aug 2007, 2009).
3. **Carry:** time-varying risk premia — KMPV reject generalized UIP/Expectations Hypothesis; carry is compensation for global-recession, liquidity, and volatility exposure (carry loses in recessions across *all* asset classes — the premium pays for bad-times losses).
4. **Quality:** AFP's puzzle — quality is underpriced relative to fundamentals ("the price of quality is positive but still quite low"); behavioral story: investors underestimate how slowly profitability mean-reverts (Asness's footnote in Siren Song); QMJ's crisis outperformance challenges pure risk stories.
5. **The ensemble itself:** value and momentum are negatively correlated (−0.5 to −0.6) with positive premia each, so the combination is "much closer to the efficient frontier than either strategy alone" (AMP) — Sharpe 1.42 for the global 50/50. Carry and quality add further low-correlation premium streams (KMPV: carry not explained by value/momentum/TSMOM; AFP: QMJ negatively correlated with HML).
6. **Why the negative correlation is structural, not luck.** Value and momentum are opposite bets on the same information: value buys what has become cheap (long-run losers), momentum buys what has been winning (recent winners). At any rebalance their portfolios are nearly mirror images in *characteristics* while both harvesting premia from different investor errors (overreaction to long-run past performance vs underreaction to recent news). This is why the correlation is reliably negative across all eight AMP markets rather than a US-stock accident — and it is also why the correlation *converges to +1 in liquidation events*: when arbitrage capital is withdrawn, both books are sold indiscriminately (AMP's funding-liquidity result). The ensemble's diversification is real in normal times and partially illusory in exactly the states where it is most needed — the single most important honest caveat in this document, and the reason the leverage cap and circuit breaker exist.

**Contradictory / decay / crowding evidence.**
- Implementation shortfall: Arnott-Kalesnik-Wu's halved value premium and vanished momentum premium in live funds (§2.4) — the single most important caution for this strategy. Mitigants: our ensemble is futures-based (deep, cheap, no single-stock shorting costs), and we adopt KMPV's carry1-12-style signal smoothing to cut turnover.
- Crowding crashes: Aug 2007 quant quake (factors crashed without valuation warning, then recovered within weeks — Asness); AMP's own funding-liquidity results imply synchronized factor losses when arbitrage capital withdraws.
- Regime dependence: carry's three great drawdowns (1972–75, 1980–82, 2008–09) are macro disasters; momentum crashes at sharp reversals (2009); value's 2017–2020 deep drawdown is common knowledge (not from a fetched source — flagged).
- Data mining: Arnott et al. and Asness both invoke it; AMP's defense is out-of-sample breadth (8 asset classes, 4 equity regions), KMPV's is cross-asset replication including options/Treasuries, AFP's is 24 countries.
- Post-publication decay: the primary papers' samples end 2011–2012; the 2012–2026 window is genuinely out-of-sample for all of them and includes the 2017–2020 value winter and the 2022 factor rotation. The desk should treat the post-2012 test window not as a formality but as the main event: if the premia were substantially revaluation or crowding (Arnott's hypothesis), that window is where it shows. Pre-register the expectation that live Sharpe will be *lower* than published backtest Sharpe — KMPV's own net-of-cost haircuts and AKW's implementation evidence both point that way — and judge the strategy against an absolute hurdle (T-bills + 5% net), not against the papers' headline numbers.

## 4. The twist: FLEX-ENS

The published ensemble is static: equal or inverse-vol weights, always on, full size in every regime. Three modifications, each tied to a documented weakness. Design constraint inherited from the timing debate (§2.4): every conditioning rule must be *bounded* (multipliers between 0.25× and 1×, never 0× or −1×), *slow* (monthly application, hysteresis on state changes), and *asymmetric across sleeves* where the literature says factor fragility is asymmetric. We are not timing factors; we are governing their risk budgets — the distinction Asness draws between the "siren song" (all-in/all-out valuation timing) and "sinning a little" (small, bounded, rules-based tilts).

**(a) Two-state vol-regime classifier per sleeve.**
*Weakness addressed:* factor losses cluster in high-vol states, but asymmetrically — carry and value are hurt most (carry's recession/liquidity exposure, KMPV §2.2; value's negative funding-liquidity loading, AMP), while momentum and quality are comparatively resilient (momentum's positive liquidity loading; QMJ's flight-to-quality convexity). A blunt "reduce everything when vol is high" throws away the sleeves that historically earn through crises.
*Rule:* for each sleeve `s` (value, momentum, carry, quality), compute trailing 60-day realized vol of the sleeve return series, `σ_s,t`. State = HIGH if `σ_s,t` exceeds the **80th percentile of its own trailing 3-year distribution**, else NORMAL. In HIGH state: **halve carry and value sleeve weights; keep momentum and quality at full weight**. In NORMAL: all sleeves at base weights. Hysteresis: a sleeve must spend 10 consecutive trading days back below the 70th percentile before returning to NORMAL (anti-whipsaw).

**(b) Crowding/valuation overlay (the Arnott governor, bounded per Asness).**
*Weakness addressed:* ABKW/Arnott — factors whose long-short valuation spreads are historically extreme have disappointing forward returns; Asness — this is only actionable at *unprecedented* extremes. We therefore act only at the top decile, and only by halving (never zeroing, never shorting the factor).
*Rule:* monthly, compute each sleeve's crowding proxy:
- Value: valuation spread of long vs short side (B/P ratio-of-ratios per AFKL/Asness; for non-stock asset classes use the 5-year-return spread between long and short baskets).
- Momentum: short-interest/borrow-based proxy where available (single-stock sleeve) or the momentum long-short basket's valuation spread (expensive winners vs cheap losers); plus 12-month sleeve return drawdown from peak as a crash-state indicator.
- Carry: cross-sectional dispersion of carry (compressed dispersion = crowded, per the logic that crowding compresses the premium).
- Quality: AFP's price-of-quality series (cross-sectional P/B-on-quality coefficient) where computable; else the QMJ long-short P/B spread.
If a sleeve's proxy sits in the **top decile of its own full trailing history (min 5 years)**, halve that sleeve's weight for the month. Only one halving per sleeve (overlays (a) and (b) compound multiplicatively to a minimum of 0.25×).

**(c) Constant ex-ante portfolio vol with inverse-vol sleeve weights.**
*Weakness addressed:* static 50/50 (AMP) lets the ensemble's vol and factor mix drift with the environment; KMPV's and AMP's own vol-scaling across asset classes is the published precedent for risk-based (not dollar-based) combination.
*Rule:* base sleeve weights `w_s ∝ 1/σ_s` (trailing 60-day vol, floored at its 3-year 25th percentile), normalized, then multiplied by the (a)/(b) state multipliers, then the whole ensemble scaled to a **10% ex-ante annualized vol target** using the trailing 60-day sleeve covariance matrix with 50% shrinkage toward equal-correlation. Gross leverage cap **2.5×**; monthly rebalancing with 20% relative drift bands per sleeve.

**Worked assembly example (illustrative arithmetic, not a backtest).** Suppose trailing 60-day sleeve vols: VALUE 9%, MOMENTUM 11%, CARRY 7%, QUALITY 6%. Inverse-vol base weights ∝ (1/9, 1/11, 1/7, 1/6) = (0.111, 0.091, 0.143, 0.167), normalized → (0.217, 0.177, 0.279, 0.326). Now the states: CARRY's vol is above its 80th percentile (HIGH state → ×0.5), and VALUE's valuation spread is in its top decile (crowding → ×0.5); MOMENTUM and QUALITY are NORMAL and uncrowded (×1). Multiplied weights: (0.108, 0.177, 0.139, 0.326), renormalized → (0.144, 0.236, 0.186, 0.434). Note what the rules did: the two crisis-resilient sleeves now carry 67% of the risk budget; carry and value are cut but *not zeroed* (the overlays never take a view that a premium is gone, only that its current risk/reward is poor). Finally, compute the ensemble's ex-ante vol from the shrunk 60-day covariance; if it reads 7.5%, scale all positions by 10/7.5 = 1.33× (within the 2.5× gross cap). If the classifier flips CARRY back to NORMAL next month (10 days below the 70th percentile), its multiplier returns to 1 at the next monthly rebalance — state changes apply only at rebalance dates, never mid-month, except the 15% drawdown circuit breaker.

## 5. Full specification of the twist variant

**Universe (futures implementation, all cross-sectional long-short within asset class).**
- Equity index futures: 18 developed markets per AMP (ES, NQ, FTSE, DAX, CAC, Nikkei, TOPIX, ASX, etc.).
- Bond futures: 10 markets per AMP (US 10y, Bund, Gilt, JGB, etc.).
- FX: 10 currencies vs USD per AMP (AUD, CAD, EUR/ DEM-spliced, JPY, NZD, NOK, SEK, CHF, GBP).
- Commodity futures: 27 contracts per AMP (energy, metals, ags, livestock).
- Optional single-stock sleeve: US + developed large-caps (top 90% market cap) for QMJ and stock value/momentum; can be deferred to phase 2.

**Signal definitions (exactly as published, §2).**
- Value: stocks/indices BE/ME (book lagged 6m); FX/bonds/commodities: −(5-year return), FX adjusted for 3m rate differentials; bonds augmented with real yield and term spread (KMPV/AMP Panel C evidence that these dominate the 5-year measure for bonds — use the composite).
- Momentum: MOM2-12 everywhere (12-month return skipping last month).
- Carry: `C = (S − F)/F` per instrument: FX → forward discount; bonds → slope + roll-down; commodities → annualized basis (nearest vs next-nearest liquid contract); equity indices → implied dividend yield from index futures basis.
- Quality (stock sleeve only): `z(Profitability + Growth + Safety + Payout)` per §2.3; futures sleeves have no quality leg (quality is equity-specific in the literature — do not improvise a macro quality factor).

**Sleeve construction.** Within each factor × asset class: rank-weighted long-short `w_i = c·(rank(S_i) − mean_rank)`, $1 long/$1 short (AMP eq. 1). Sleeve = inverse-vol combination of the factor's asset-class sub-portfolios. Four sleeves: VALUE, MOMENTUM, CARRY, QUALITY (QUALITY = stock sleeve only; the other three span all futures asset classes).

**Ensemble assembly.** Per §4(c): inverse-vol base weights × regime multiplier (§4a) × crowding multiplier (§4b) → scale to 10% ex-ante vol, cap 2.5× gross. Rebalance monthly; drift band 20% relative per sleeve; signal smoothing: carry uses the carry1-12 trailing-average variant (KMPV: Sharpe 1.1, ~50% less turnover); momentum and value rebalance monthly per AMP (annual rebalancing produced similar results in AMP's robustness check — a turnover lever if needed).

**Entries/exits.** Continuously invested; no trade-level entries/exits. De-risking events: (a)/(b) multipliers; portfolio-level circuit breaker — if ensemble drawdown exceeds **15%**, cut gross to 1.25× and re-review the whole spec before re-levering (governance rule, not literature).

**Costs (assumptions for validation, not literature claims).** Futures: 1–4 bp/side by asset class plus roll costs at actuals; FX forwards: 1–3 bp; stock sleeve: 15–30 bp/side including borrow on shorts, with a 2× stress case; financing at T-bill + 25bp on net and gross exposure as applicable. Benchmark the stock-sleeve cost model against Arnott-Kalesnik-Wu's slippage findings — if our assumed costs can't reproduce at least half of their momentum shortfall in a replication, the cost model is wrong, not the factor.

**Phasing.** Phase 1: futures-only ensemble (VALUE, MOMENTUM, CARRY across the four futures asset classes) — fully implementable with public data, no borrow, no single-stock microstructure. Phase 2: add the stock sleeve and the QUALITY factor once CRSP/Compustat/XpressFeed and short-interest data are licensed and the §7 replication protocol has passed for QMJ. The classifier and crowding overlay are specified identically in both phases; in phase 1, QUALITY simply has zero weight (do not substitute a macro proxy — §5 signal table). This phasing is deliberate: the stock sleeve is where implementation risk (borrow, delistings, capacity, data look-ahead) concentrates, and the futures ensemble can be validated while that infrastructure is built.

**Capacity.** Futures sleeves: $1bn+ without material impact (deep macro markets). Stock sleeve: capacity-limited by small-cap liquidity and borrow; keep single-name positions < 5% of ADV and accept that capacity is the price of the quality leg. KMPV note net-of-cost significance survives for all futures asset classes; options carry (their highest-cost sleeve) is excluded from our spec for exactly that reason.

## 6. Failure modes & regime dependence

**What kills it.**
1. **Funding-liquidity crisis (Aug 2007, Mar 2020).** AMP's central economic finding is that value and momentum load oppositely on funding liquidity — in a funding shock, momentum crashes while value merely suffers; crowded unwinds hit both when leverage is forced down indiscriminately. Aug 2007: extreme factor losses for ~a week, mostly recovered within weeks (Asness's account). Our vol-regime classifier (a) reacts in ~60-day windows — too slow for a one-week quake; the defense is the leverage cap and the fact that we never run the levered crowded single-stock momentum book that died in 2007.
2. **Momentum reversal crashes (2009-type).** After a bear-market trough, the short leg (past losers, now junk rallying hardest) detonates. Skipping the most recent month (MOM2-12) and the vol-regime halving of *value* (not momentum) in high-vol states means we deliberately keep momentum exposure through the early recovery — this is a published-evidence-based choice (momentum's positive liquidity loading), not an oversight, but 2009-style pain is the accepted cost.
3. **Carry recessions (1972–75, 1980–82, 2008–09 per KMPV).** All carry strategies across asset classes lose together in global recessions — the modest unconditional correlations mask low-frequency co-movement. Classifier (a) halves carry in high-vol states, which historically coincide with these episodes; the lag means the first month of the drawdown is taken at full size.
4. **Value winter (prolonged growth/quality-led markets).** Multi-year value underperformance (2017–2020, common knowledge — not a fetched source) grinds the VALUE sleeve; the crowding overlay (b) is designed to be *long*-biased protection (it trims expensive factors, not cheap ones) and will not save a cheap-but-falling value factor. Asness's honest answer — valuation didn't predict Aug 2007 and won't predict value winters — applies to us: we survive via diversification and the vol target, not foresight.
5. **Classifier whipsaw.** 60-day vol crossing its 80th percentile repeatedly in choppy-but-not-crisis markets (e.g., 2011, 2015–16) will halve/reinstate sleeves at a cost. The 10-day hysteresis and 70th-percentile exit threshold are the mitigation; validate the cost explicitly (§7).
6. **Crowding-proxy look-ahead and data sparsity.** Short-interest and borrow data are short-history; the price-of-quality series needs full cross-sectional accounting data. Where a proxy has < 5 years of history, the overlay is inert for that sleeve by construction (min-history rule) — document this rather than backfilling.
7. **Ensemble-level correlation convergence.** The whole design rests on value-momentum correlation ≈ −0.5 (AMP) and low carry/quality correlations. In a true liquidity spiral these correlations move toward +1 for the *losses* (everything an arbitrageur holds gets sold). The 10% vol target rescales using a 60-day covariance that will not see the convergence coming; the 2.5× gross cap and the 15% circuit breaker are the only defenses that do not depend on estimated correlations. Size the sleeve so that a 2.5×-levered week of converged-factor losses is survivable at the portfolio level — that number, not the Sharpe, is the real capacity constraint.
8. **Signal-convention drift.** Each factor has a dozen defensible implementations (book-to-price vs composite value; rank vs z-score weights; skip-month or not; carry window). The published results are robust across these choices *on average*, but any single choice can dominate in a given decade. The defense is to fix conventions to the primary papers (§5 table) and change them only through the robustness protocol — never mid-drawdown. A related trap: data-vendor "improvements" (restatements, universe changes) silently changing signals; pin vendor versions in the replication environment.

**Regime dependence summary.** The ensemble earns in "normal" regimes where arbitrage capital is ample and factor premia are paid steadily; it suffers in (i) funding-liquidity crises (fast, days-weeks: Aug 2007, Mar 2020), (ii) macro disaster regimes where carry and value bleed for quarters (1972–75, 1980–82, 2008–09 per KMPV), (iii) sharp reversal rallies that detonate momentum (2009), and (iv) long growth-led grinds that starve value (2017–2020, flagged as non-fetched common knowledge). The overlays are matched to these: (a) handles the slow macro regimes, (b) handles the crowded extremes, the vol target handles ordinary turbulence, and the leverage cap plus circuit breaker handle the fast quakes that no monthly process can see coming. No overlay predicts; all are damage-limiters.

## 7. Validation protocol

**Data needs.** Futures: daily prices for all contracts per AMP/KMPV universes (CSI/Datastream/ Bloomberg), with roll schedules; FX spot+forward points; bond yields + inflation forecasts (Consensus Economics per AMP) for the bond value composite; commodities spot series for 5-year value; stock sleeve: CRSP/Compustat + XpressFeed Global (AFP's exact sources), including delisting returns (AFP assume −30% for missing performance-related delistings, following Shumway 1997 — adopt the same convention). Short interest/borrow: Markit/IHS or exchange biweekly data (2006+). Macro: none required beyond signals.

**Chronological splits.** Development: 1972–1995 (build signals, fix estimator choices). Validation: 1996–2011 (matches AMP's sample end; includes 1998 LTCM, 2000–02, 2007 quake, 2008–09). Test: 2012–2026 (fully post-publication for AMP 2013/QMJ 2013; includes 2018, 2020, 2022, and the 2017–2020 value winter). The post-2012 window is the honest one: every primary paper predates it.

**Cost / roll / financing model.** Per §5; futures rolls at actual calendar-spread costs; FX at forward points; stock sleeve with borrow at actuals where available, else 50bp/yr default + 2× stress. Report gross and net side by side; a factor ensemble that only works gross is a negative result (Arnott's lesson).

**Strategy-specific pitfalls.**
- *Signal timestamping:* book values lagged 6 months (AMP convention); accounting aligned to June of year t for fiscal year t−1 (AFP convention); momentum skip-month implemented exactly (off-by-one month is a classic silent bug).
- *Futures roll ambiguity:* which contract is "the" price series (nearest vs most-liquid) changes both momentum and carry; fix AMP's "most liquid, typically nearest or next-nearest" rule and never mix.
- *Rank-weight vs tercile:* AMP's factors use rank weights; tercile spreads are the robustness check, not the reverse.
- *Sleeve-vol estimation:* the classifier's 60-day vol of *sleeve returns* requires a long simulated sleeve history before any live date — build the full sleeve series first, then classify; never use full-sample percentiles.
- *Crowding overlay history:* top-decile rules need ≥5 years of proxy history; do not let the overlay "work" in 2010 with a proxy that starts in 2009.
- *Survivorship in the stock sleeve:* delisting returns included; universe re-derived monthly (top-90%-cap rule), not today's survivors.

**Robustness checks.** Classifier: window {40, 60, 120} days × threshold {70th, 80th, 90th pct} × exit {65th, 70th, 75th}; crowding decile vs quintile trigger; vol target {8, 10, 12%}; sleeve set ablations (drop each factor once; drop the stock sleeve); momentum skip {0, 1} months (AMP note non-stock momentum is *stronger* without the skip — test both, report both); carry vs carry1-12. Report the full grid; the spec must not be a grid optimum.

**Replication-first protocol (mandatory before any twist evaluation).** Step 1: rebuild AMP's value and momentum factors in all eight asset classes over their sample and match their published signs and approximate magnitudes (value/momentum positive in ≥7 of 8 classes, value-momentum correlation negative, COMBO Sharpe in the 1.0–1.5 ballpark gross). Step 2: rebuild KMPV's carry factor per asset class and the diversified carry (target: sign-correct everywhere, diversified Sharpe in the 0.8–1.4 ballpark gross). Step 3: rebuild QMJ in the US (sign-correct, positive alpha, crisis outperformance qualitatively). Only after all three replications pass within tolerance do we evaluate FLEX-ENS overlays; a stack that cannot reproduce its ancestors' published results proves a data or construction bug, not a discovery. Document all replication deltas — even the ones we cannot explain; unexplained deltas cap the trust we place in overlay results.

**Acceptance criteria (pre-registered).** (i) Net-of-cost ensemble Sharpe positive in each split, with the test window decisive; (ii) ensemble max DD ≤ 60% of the worst single-sleeve DD (diversification must actually show up); (iii) the (a)+(b) overlays must improve net Sharpe or max DD vs the static inverse-vol ensemble in validation — if not, ship the static version and document the negative result; (iv) replicate AMP's headline signs before trusting our own stack: value and momentum positive in ≥7 of 8 asset classes, value-momentum correlation negative, carry positive in all futures asset classes; (v) live-paper-trade 6 months before capital; compare realized slippage to the cost model and recalibrate once, documented.

**Benchmarks & reporting standards.** Report against: (1) the static 50/50 value-momentum combo (AMP's published combination — the direct ancestor); (2) the static inverse-vol four-sleeve ensemble with no overlays (isolates what (a)+(b) add); (3) each sleeve standalone (sleeves must individually earn or be cut); (4) T-bills + 5% as an absolute hurdle. Report gross and net side by side always (Arnott's lesson: a factor ensemble that only works gross is a negative result); turnover by sleeve; classifier state occupancy (% months in HIGH per sleeve); crowding-overlay trigger frequency and the forward returns following triggers (does the governor actually govern?); overlay P&L attribution (static vs regime vs crowding contributions); and the worst 10 days with their mechanism labels (funding crisis / reversal / macro). All figures with window, cost assumption, and benchmark set attached.

**What would falsify the strategy.** Pre-register the kill criteria: (i) the static ensemble fails to beat T-bills + 5% net in the 2012–2026 test window while the overlays also fail — the premia themselves have decayed beyond use, not just our conditioning; (ii) value-momentum correlation is no longer negative out-of-sample across the futures asset classes — the core diversification engine is gone; (iii) replication protocol fails and the deltas cannot be explained by data conventions — we never understood the ancestors. Any of these → archive the document, do not iterate parameters until the numbers improve.
